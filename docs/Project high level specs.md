# Family — Specification

This document says what Family does. It is the specification the first vertical slice is
built from.

**Status.** Family is one phase and this document specifies all of it. Family reports what
its members have and know, and does not advise anybody what they are missing or where in the
game to get it; §2.5 says why.

## 1. What Family is

The characters one makes in World of Warcraft and rotates amongst are, in a small way, part
of a life. Some people see them as children, some as siblings, some as friends. Family keeps
track of each one's life — its status, its evolution, its possessions — and, when you want
it to, of the lives of the characters belonging to people you actually play with.

Most addons call those characters *alts*. Family calls them **members**, and that word is
used consistently throughout this document and throughout the interface.

Family records what each member owns and knows while you are playing it, and shows all of it
to you while you are logged in on a different one. There is no way to ask the server about a
character you are not on; everything Family knows, it learned by watching.

### Supported clients

| Client | Supported |
|---|---|
| Classic Era | yes |
| Classic Burning Crusade Anniversary | yes |
| Classic Mists of Pandaria | yes |
| Season of Discovery | no, and not planned |
| Retail | no, and not planned |

Mists is specified here as a first-class target: every feature below states what it does on
each of the three, and the design is expansion-aware from the beginning rather than
retrofitted. The *data* that Mists needs — its craft levels — is generated by tools that
today only cover Era and Burning Crusade, and extending them is a separate, later task
(§11). Nothing in Phase 1 waits on it.

## 2. Decisions that govern everything else

These are settled. They are recorded here rather than in the sections they affect, because
each of them affects many.

### 2.1 Family stores IDs. It never stores names.

Every item, spell, quest, faction and achievement in the game has a numeric identifier that
is the same in every language, and a name that is not. Family writes the identifier and
nothing else. Names are asked of the client at the moment something is drawn, so what you
read is always in the language you are playing in.

This is what makes §8 work at no cost, and it is what makes a link with a player running a
German client (§6) produce readable data rather than German strings embedded permanently in
your saved file. It is also the single hardest decision here to reverse: adding a name cache
later is an afternoon's work, and removing names from a format that has already shipped to
users is a migration.

The rule generalises past storage, and [`DATASOURCES.md`](DATASOURCES.md) §1 states the
general form: **ask the client first, by id, and store ids rather than words.** A name held
as a word is one language and goes stale; a name held as an id is eleven languages and is
whatever Blizzard currently calls it. Where the client can answer, nothing else is asked and
nothing else is shipped. Third-party name tables are the fallback for the handful of things
the client has no id for, not the first stop.

Two consequences to build for rather than be surprised by:

- **A name may not be available the instant it is wanted.** The client answers about an item
  only once it has loaded that item. Family therefore has a name-resolution layer that asks
  for what it is missing, fills it in when the answer arrives, and shows the identifier in
  the meantime rather than showing nothing.
- **Search matches names, so there is a name index.** It is built from identifiers, it is
  valid for one language only, and it is discarded and rebuilt if the client language
  changes.

A cache of resolved names, stored alongside the identifiers, is a safe optimisation to add
later if drawing or searching proves too slow in practice. It is not part of the format now.

**Talents once were the exception, and are not any more.** The client will only answer about
*your own class's* talents: there is no call that asks what the third talent in a warlock's
second tree is called while you are playing a mage, and showing another member's talents is
the whole point of §4.4. So a tree talent was stored with its name beside its position, and a
member recorded on a German client showed German talent names on an English one.

What settles it is that **a talent is a spell**, and the client answers about any spell id for
any class without loading anything first. `addons/Family/TalentSpells.lua`, generated from the
client's own `Talent` and `TalentTab` tables by `tools/talents.py`, maps a talent's position —
class, tree, tier, column — to the spell id of its first rank, and the reader's own client
names it. That is every language the game ships in rather than the five Family writes, and no
names are shipped at all.

It is keyed by expansion, because Era and Burning Crusade hold different talents at thirty-two
of the four hundred and nineteen positions they share. Mists needs none of it: its
specialisations already have ids that resolve for any class.

The recorded name is kept as the fallback, for a record made before that table existed and for
a position no table knows. Nothing needs rescanning.

### 2.2 Nothing is shown that was never seen

Family's honesty about its own gaps is a feature, not an apology. A bank that has never been
opened is reported as *not visited*, not as empty. A profession cooldown that started while
the trade skill window was shut is not known until that window is next opened, and the
interface says *no cooldown seen* rather than *no cooldown*. Every recorded value carries
the time it was recorded, and anything that can be stale says how old it is.

This principle decides a great many small interface questions, so it is stated once here.

### 2.3 Expansion capability is data, not branching

Whether a feature exists is a property of the client, held in one capability table, and
asked rather than inferred. Achievements, currencies, daily-quest tracking, guild banks,
keyrings and ammunition bags each exist on some clients and not others.

**A feature the client does not have is absent, not empty.** No tab, no column, no grid, no
option — rather than a control that opens onto nothing.

The table below is the working set. Rows marked ✓ have been confirmed against a running
client; the rest are still expectations.

| Capability | Era | Burning Crusade | Mists |
|---|---|---|---|
| Guild bank | no | yes ✓ | yes |
| Daily quest tracking | no | yes | yes |
| Achievements | no | **no ✓** | yes |
| Currencies | no | **yes ✓** | yes |
| Dual specialisation | yes | yes ✓ | yes |
| Talent trees (point spend) | yes | yes | no — tiered choices |
| Glyphs | no | no | yes |
| Keyring | yes | yes ✓ | no |
| Ammunition and soul bags | yes | yes | no |
| Attunements | yes | yes | vestigial |
| Transmogrification, void storage | no | no | yes |
| Account-wide mounts and pets | no | no | yes |

**Dual specialisation is the row that catches people out, and it is the reason this table
exists rather than being inferred from expansion history.** Two specialisations were a Wrath
feature originally, so a table derived from what each expansion originally shipped would say
*no* for Era and Burning Crusade. It has since been added to both, and it is present on all
three clients Family targets. **Every member on every client can therefore have two talent
configurations**, and nothing in this document may treat a second one as a Mists-only case.

That is the general lesson: what these clients have is what Blizzard has given them since,
not what the expansion shipped with eighteen years ago. Verify against the running client,
never against the history. **Anniversary confirmed both halves of it on 2026-08-08:** dual
specialisation is present, and so is the currency system, with Honor carried as a currency.
Neither shipped with Burning Crusade.

**But asking the client is not the same as asking about the feature.** The same session
found Anniversary carrying `GetAchievementInfo` while having no achievements at all — the
function is in the client because the client is built from one codebase, and its presence
says nothing about whether the feature is in the game. So capability detection asks about
behaviour where the client offers such a question, and where it does not it asks nothing and
falls back to this table. A researched expectation beats a confident wrong answer.

**And a function that exists may still refuse to run.** Anniversary carries
`GetNumSpecGroups` and answers *API unsupported in this version of World of Warcraft* the
moment it is called. So `if SomeFunction then` is not merely uninformative on these clients,
it is a trap: the guard passes and the call it was protecting brings the scan down. Any call
not present on all three clients is therefore **attempted, not tested for**, with failure
falling through to the next candidate. Three distinct ways for existence to mislead have now
been found — a symbol without its feature, a feature without its symbol, and a symbol that
throws — which is why the table above is the authority and the client is not.

### 2.4 Two addons, one saved file

`Family` records and stores. `Family_UI` draws. Everything else is a file and a namespace
inside one of those two. There is one saved variable, `FamilyDB`, carrying a schema version
and a migration path that runs on load.

Member records are stored serialized and compressed, and decoded on demand for the member
actually being looked at — not all of them, at every login, whether or not you open the
window. On an account with forty members that is the largest single cost there is, and it is
designed out rather than optimised later.

### 2.5 One phase. Family reports; it does not advise

**Decided 2026-08-09.**

Family shows what its members have and know. It does not tell anybody what they are missing,
what they should get next, or where in the game to go and get it. Specifically, and by name,
Family will not answer:

- which recipes a member still lacks for a profession
- which piece of gear a member should improve next
- where an item is looted, sold, quest-rewarded or otherwise obtained

Every one of those needs facts the client does not hold. **The game client knows what a thing
*is*; it does not know where a thing *comes from*, because that lives on the server.** Any
addon that answers those questions is reading a catalogue somebody compiled outside the game
— which brings a licence to honour, a dataset to keep current across three clients and eleven
languages, and answers whose staleness Family would have no way to state. All three are the
opposite of how the rest of this document works (§2.1, §2.2).

So the boundary is not a schedule slipping; it is the shape of the addon. **Everything Family
says, it says because the client said it, or because another Family said it (§6).** Nothing is
inferred from a table shipped in the box.

The generators in `tools/` that read the *client's own* tables remain legitimate: those are
the client's facts, taken out of game for convenience rather than compiled by a third
party.

## 3. What Family records

Recorded for every member, while that member is played, and kept for reading from any other.

### Identity and standing

| | Notes |
|---|---|
| Name, realm, faction, race, class, gender, level | |
| Which specialisation is active, and when it was last switched | all clients — see §2.3 |
| Guild name and rank | |
| Money | |
| Time played, total and at level | |
| Experience and rested experience | |
| Hearthstone location | |
| Last seen online, as a date and time | |

### Possessions

| | Notes |
|---|---|
| Every bag and its contents | |
| The bank and its contents | recorded when the bank is opened |
| The guild bank | Burning Crusade onwards, and only while the member is in a guild |
| Total and free bag space, total and free bank space | |
| Equipped gear, and average item level | |
| Keys | Era and Burning Crusade; the keyring is gone by Mists |

**Special bags are counted separately and never as general capacity.** A quiver holds
ammunition, a soul bag holds shards, a profession bag holds its own trade goods and nothing
else. Sixteen slots of quiver is not sixteen slots of room, and reporting it as such
overstates what a member can carry. Special bags are listed individually wherever bags are
broken down, with their type; they are excluded from the free-slot totals.

### Activity

| | Notes |
|---|---|
| Mailbox contents, and the expiry of each item | recorded when a mailbox is opened |
| Mail posted from one member to another | recorded against the recipient as it is sent (§5) |
| Date of the last mailbox visit | |
| Active auctions and bids | recorded when the auction house is opened |
| Date of the last auction house visit | |
| Instance lockouts and their expiry | |

### Knowledge

| | Notes |
|---|---|
| Learnt abilities and spells | |
| Talents | the full build of **both specialisations**, on every client (§2.3) |
| Glyphs | Mists |
| Primary professions, secondary professions, and their ranks | only those the client has |
| Every learnt recipe, with its difficulty colour | recorded when the profession window is opened |
| Crafting cooldowns, on recipes and on the items that go with them | as seen — see §2.2. Called **crafting** cooldowns everywhere they are reported, so that the absence of instance lockouts below is read as an absence rather than as a promise |
| Quests currently in the log | |
| Quest completion history | |
| Daily quests completed | Burning Crusade onwards |
| Reputations | only the factions the client has |
| Achievements | Mists |
| Currencies | Mists |
| Attunement progress | Era and Burning Crusade |

Everything in the *Knowledge* and *Possessions* tables is stored as identifiers (§2.1).

### When recording happens

Most of it is read at login and whenever the game announces a change. Three things can only
be read while a window is open, and are therefore as old as the last time you opened it:
the **bank**, the **guild bank**, and a **profession's recipe list and cooldowns**. The
**mailbox** and the **auction house** are the same, and both carry a visible last-visited
date for that reason.

Bags are the exception in the other direction: they are rescanned shortly after every login
and again on every change, so bag data is effectively current.

## 4. The window

Opened with `/family`, or `/fam`. One window, tabs down the side.

The interface is designed for **dozens of members across several realms** as the normal
case, not the extreme one. Grouping, collapsing and filtering are load-bearing, not
decoration.

### 4.1 Summary

The main table: one row per member, grouped by realm and by account, with a totals line.
Realms and accounts collapse and expand.

The left menu switches between column sets:

| Set | Columns |
|---|---|
| **Overview** | level, rested experience, money, time played, average item level, last online |
| **Bags** | bag capacity, free bag slots, bank capacity, free bank slots |
| **Professions** | both primary professions plus cooking, first aid and fishing, each with current and maximum rank |
| **Activity** | mail count and last mailbox visit, auctions, bids, last auction house visit |
| **Miscellaneous** | guild, hearthstone location, class, specialisation |

First aid does not exist on every client, and the *Professions* set follows §2.3 rather than
showing an always-empty column.

The bag and bank columns show one number per container. Hovering gives the full breakdown,
one bag per line with its name, its size, how many slots are free, and — for a special bag —
what it is restricted to.

Hovering a profession gives its recipe counts by difficulty, and the cooldowns that have
been seen for it.

Every column header sorts on that column. Right-clicking gives a menu whose contents depend
on what was clicked: on a member's row, actions for that member including deleting the
record held for it; on a realm or account header, the same for everything beneath it.
Deletion concerns Family's records, never anything in the game.

### 4.2 The recap

Above the table, a synthesis across the whole family: total money, total free space, how
many members have mail waiting, how many have auctions about to end, how many have a
profession cooldown ready, and how many have not been played in a long time.

This is the panel that answers *what needs my attention today* without reading forty rows,
and it is the first thing the window shows.

### 4.3 Members

One member at a time, chosen from a list that is searchable rather than a row of icons —
because forty members do not fit in a row of icons. The panels are:

| Panel | Shows |
|---|---|
| **Containers** | every bag, the bank, and the guild bank, drawn as they appear in game. Filter and search within them by name, type and quality. |
| **Equipment** | what the member is wearing, slot by slot — and, in the other mode, what the whole family is wearing at once (below) |
| **Spellbook** | every learnt ability, by school, with a search box |
| **Professions** | one profession's recipes, filtered by difficulty colour, by equipment slot, and by name. Searching matches the name of the item the recipe makes, or the name of the spell for recipes that make no item. |
| **Talents** | both specialisations, side by side, on every client. Glyphs alongside, on Mists. |
| **Quests** | the quest log by zone, with filters, and the completion history |
| **Auctions** | that member's auctions and bids, with filters |
| **Mail** | what is in the mailbox and when each item expires |
| **Reputations** | standing with every faction the client has |

### 4.3.1 Everyone's gear at once

The equipment panel has a second mode, and it answers a different question. One member at a
time, drawn as a character sheet, answers *what is this character wearing*. The whole family
at once answers *who is behind*, which is the question an alt manager exists for and which
no character sheet can be made to answer however many times it is opened.

In this mode each member is **one row**, and a row is a sequence of icons:

- **First, the class icon**, hovered for who they are: name, race, class, level, average item
  level.
- **Then every equipped slot**, in the order the game's own character sheet puts them, each
  drawn with the item's own icon, its item level printed over it, and the item's tooltip on
  hover. An empty slot is drawn with the game's own picture for that slot, so a missing
  helmet is a gap in the same place on every row rather than a word.

Siblings (§6) appear here too, so "the whole family" means the same thing it means
everywhere else in the window.

Filters on **realm** and on **class**. Both are lists of what is actually there rather than
of everything the game has: a family with no warlock is not offered a warlock filter.

The single-member sheet is not replaced by this and does not become a special case of it.
They are two readings of the same record, and the panel switches between them.

### 4.4 Talent comparison

Any two members of the family, their talent builds drawn side by side, differences marked.
This includes members belonging to a linked family (§6), which is the case it is really for:
comparing your build against someone else's is a conversation people have constantly and
have no tool for.

**Comparison is per specialisation, on every client**, and which specialisation is being
compared is part of the choice — so a member's off-spec can be compared against another
member's main, which is usually the interesting question. Since both specialisations exist
everywhere (§2.3), this is never a Mists-only refinement.

A specialisation that has never been visited has nothing recorded for it, and is offered as
such rather than drawn as an empty tree (§2.2).

### 4.5 Search

One search box, one *where* to search, one *what* to search.

**Where** — this member, this realm and faction, this realm both factions, all realms, all
accounts, the whole family including linked families.

**What** — bags, bank, guild bank, mail, and active auctions. Results say which member holds
the item, where, and how many.

`/family search <name>` runs the search from chat against the currently selected *where*.
The tab makes plain which one that is before the results are read.

### 4.6 Grids

One subject at a time, every member side by side.

| Grid | Shows | Clients |
|---|---|---|
| **Equipment** | what everyone is wearing, slot by slot | all |
| **Reputations** | standing with every faction | all |
| **Professions** | professions and ranks across the family | all |
| **Keys** | dungeon keys and keyring contents | Era, Burning Crusade |
| **Attunements** | attunement progress | Era, Burning Crusade |
| **Daily quests** | which dailies each member has done | Burning Crusade onwards |
| **Currencies** | tokens and currencies | Mists |
| **Achievements** | achievement progress | Mists |

Grids for capabilities the client lacks are absent, per §2.3.

### 4.7 Calendar

A month view of what every member has coming up. Clicking a day lists that day's entries.

| | |
|---|---|
| Profession cooldowns | per member, and the ones a whole profession shares |
| Instance lockouts | when each expires |
| Item cooldowns | things in bags on a timer |
| Mail expiry | anything approaching the point at which it is returned or destroyed |
| Calendar events | invitations from the in-game calendar, where the client has one |

**Warnings.** A master switch, and then one independent list of lead times per kind of
event, so a transmute can nag differently from a raid lockout. Each list is a set of
checkboxes — ticking thirty minutes and five minutes gives exactly two warnings — and every
lead time is independent of every other.

A warning is a line in the chat frame, or a dialog if you prefer one; the dialog is
suppressed in combat and falls back to the chat line, because nothing Family has to say is
worth interrupting a fight.

Expiry itself is always announced regardless of lead times. Something that expired while you
were away is reported once, as having expired, rather than announced late as though it were
news.

A cooldown appears only if it was seen (§2.2).

### 4.8 Options

- **Window** — scale, transparency, minimap button, position and clamping.
- **Recording** — how much history to keep, and what to scan when.
- **Mail** — expiry warnings, recipient autocomplete, new-mail announcement.
- **Tooltips** — see §5.
- **Calendar** — the switches and lead-time lists above.
- **Wide Family** — links, what each link may see, who is a sibling, and whether exchanges
  happen automatically. See §6.
- **Guild share** — on or off, in both directions at once. See §7.

## 5. Family outside its window

Most of the time the answer is wanted where the question arises, which is not inside a
window you have to open.

**Item tooltips.** Mousing over any item anywhere in the game adds who in the family owns
one, and how many, and where — bags, bank, guild bank, mail, auction house. Members
belonging to a linked family appear here too, marked as theirs. The breakdown can be reduced
to a single total for a quieter tooltip. Item identifier and item level are available and
off by default.

**Everybody's quests at once.** The Quests section has a whole-family reading, like gear and
reputations: one line per quest with the characters who have it under it, furthest along first,
each with their own progress. Three at a time, with the rest a click away. The filter box acts
on quest names and zones; the realm, class and level filters act on the characters.

**A recipe row reads both ways.** A recipe is two things — the spell that makes something and
the item it makes — and Family's own recipe rows show what it makes. Holding **CTRL** with the
pointer on the row shows the recipe instead, and letting go puts it back, without moving the
mouse. The row says the key is there; where only one of the two is known, nothing is offered
and nothing is said.

**New mail.** Mail arriving for the member you are playing is announced in the chat frame.

**Mail nearing expiry.** Any member's mail approaching its return-or-destroy date is
announced, because the whole point is that it is *not* the member you are playing. This is
the one warning that is on by default.

**Mail sent to another member is written down as it is sent.** Posting to one of your own
characters is the one moment where Family knows something about a member's mailbox without
that member's mailbox being open — the money, the attachments and the subject are all in
front of you at the instant you press Send. So they are recorded against the *recipient*
straight away, and the recipient's row shows them before that character has ever logged in.

This is not an exception to §2.2. The letter was seen: by the sender, being sent. What is
carefully *not* claimed is that it has arrived — a sent letter is marked as in the post
until that member opens their own mailbox, at which point their own scan replaces the lot,
because a real inbox is a better fact than a prediction of one. A send that the server
refuses is never recorded at all: the record is written when the game confirms the send, not
when the button is pressed.

**Taking mail updates the record immediately**, including taking only some of it. The
mailbox scan runs on every change while the box is open and once more as it closes, so a
member who empties three letters out of eight is recorded as having five, not as having
eight until something else happens to look.

## 6. Wide Family

A family is not necessarily one account. Family lets you add another player's characters as
members of yours, and lets them add yours.

This is the feature Family exists for as much as any other, and it is designed around one
principle: **nothing is ever visible that was not deliberately made visible, one member and
one category at a time.**

### 6.0 It ships switched off, and now for a different reason

Decided 2026-08-10, when the rest of Family was judged stable enough to release and this was
not. Revised 2026-08-27, when the live check reached it.

The original gate had two conditions: a live client showing the exchange doing what the
harness says it does, and §11.1 answered by testing rather than assumption. The first is met —
the 1.0.0-beta.2 pass linked two families on all three clients, sent, received, and abandoned
an exchange interrupted by a logout. The second is not, and the checklist that was supposed to
settle it has no line that tests realm reach; see §11.1.

It ships switched off all the same, and the reason is no longer the gate. Consent is the one
thing here that cannot be repaired afterwards: a wrong tooltip is corrected in the next
version, but a member shared with somebody who was never granted them has already been shared
by the time anyone notices, and no later version takes that back. A feature whose whole
purpose is to hold a line does not arrive holding it on somebody's behalf — it waits to be
asked for. That is a position rather than a caveat, and unlike a caveat it does not expire.

It therefore ships **inert**: nothing sent, nothing that arrives acted on, no panel, no
borrowed members shown. `/family wide on`, then a reload, turns it on; both players need to.

Shipping it switched on is a separate decision, reserved to the author, and a green pass does
not take it.

### Linking

Both players run Family. One sends a **link request** to the other by character name; the
other accepts. Until accepted, nothing whatsoever has been exchanged.

A link is between two *families*, not two characters. It survives either player switching
members.

### Visibility

Once linked, each side chooses what the other may see, and the default is nothing.

- Each member is offered to the link individually, and must be approved individually.
- For each offered member, each **category** of data — possessions, equipment, professions,
  talents, quests, mail, auctions, reputations, money, character, currencies, world buffs — is
  granted or withheld separately.
  Equipment is its own category rather than part of possessions: what somebody is wearing is
  the thing most often worth showing a friend and the thing least like a list of what they
  own, and a player willing to share one is frequently unwilling to share the other.

  The last three were added on 2026-09-04, for the same reason and after the same complaint
  from play: every column they carry was one a shared character could never fill. **Character**
  is time played, rested experience, guild and hearthstone; **currencies** is the currency set;
  **world buffs** is a Chronoboon and what is in it. Kept apart rather than folded into one,
  because somebody will happily say where their alts are bound and not how long they have
  played, and a category that cannot be separated is a decision the player cannot make.
- Grants are revocable at any time. Revoking a grant deletes the data already transferred
  under it from the other side at the next contact, and marks it withdrawn in the meantime.

The interface for this is a grid: members down one side, categories across the top,
individually tickable, with a visible count of what is currently shared.

### Transfer

Data moves between two linked families:

1. **When the link is established**, for whatever is already granted.
2. **On demand**, when a member of either family asks for an update on their own client,
   while any member of the other family is online.

The member asking for an update **need not be one of the visible members**. Any member of
your family may trigger a refresh; what comes back is exactly what has been granted,
concerning exactly the members that have been approved. The two facts are unrelated on
purpose — the person at the keyboard is asking, not the character.

Transfer is bidirectional: an update brings their granted data to you and takes your granted
data to them in the same exchange.

### Exchanging without being asked

**Added 2026-08-09.** On demand is the floor, not the ceiling. Two families that have agreed
to share should not have to remember to press a button.

- **On login**, once the session has settled, each side announces itself to every family it
  is linked to. Anyone who hears it exchanges. Neither end polls and neither end has to be
  asked, so two people playing at the same time stay current with each other for nothing.
- **When a grant changes**, at once. Taking a grant away is the half that has to be prompt:
  waiting for somebody to press Update would mean the other side kept it for as long as
  nobody did.
- **On demand**, still, from the panel.

**All of that is one switch, and it can be turned off.** *Exchange automatically* covers both
halves — announcing yourself when you log in, and answering somebody else's announcement
while you play. Off means neither happens and no exchange is ever begun by anything but a
person: the panel's *Update now* remains, and **on demand is never switched off with it**, because it
is the floor the whole feature stands on rather than a convenience on top of it.

One thing crosses the switch, and it crosses it deliberately: **withdrawing a grant is still
sent at once.** Automatic update is a convenience, and a player who does not want their
evening interrupted by transfers is entitled to switch it off. Telling somebody to forget
what they may no longer see is not a convenience — it is the promise the grid is made of, and
a promise that waits for the next time somebody presses a button is not one. So the switch
governs what Family sends for your benefit, and not what it sends for the other player's.

**On logout, nothing is sent, and that is deliberate.** The moment an addon learns it is
logging out, the client is already leaving; a whisper posted then is not delivered, and a
transfer of any size certainly does not finish. Trying anyway would produce a feature that
appears to work and silently does not — the one shape this specification refuses everywhere
else. The login announcement covers the same ground honestly: the next time either of you
plays, both sides are brought up to date.

**Combat.** Sending is not forbidden in combat, which is worth stating because it is
frequently assumed to be. What is true is that the addon channel is shared and rate limited,
so a large transfer during a fight competes with the raid's own addons for something they
need more than Family does. Bulk therefore waits for the fight to end and control messages do
not — a reply that waits is a reply nobody connects to what they asked.

### What Wide Family requires

The addon channel carries a string and nothing else, so Wide Family cannot work without
serialisation: `LibSerialize` and `LibDeflate`. Everywhere else in Family those libraries are
optional and their absence costs only compression. Here their absence costs the feature, and
the panel says so outright rather than offering a link that never works.

### What crosses the wire

The payload identifies every table **by name** and carries a **schema version**. Two players
running different versions of Family degrade to *that table cannot be read here*, naming the
table and both versions. They never silently mis-parse each other's data. This is stated as
a requirement rather than left to the implementation because getting it wrong corrupts
records rather than failing visibly.

Identifiers cross the wire, never names (§2.1), so a link between a French client and a
German one is not merely possible but unremarkable.

### What linked data is, and is not

- It is **stored separately** from your own members and always marked as belonging to
  another family. It is never merged, and it is never edited.
- It is a **snapshot**, refreshed when asked. It does not subscribe.
- It is **as old as the last exchange**, and every panel says so.
- Both players must be **online at once** for an exchange, because the transport is the game's
  own addon messaging.

### Siblings

A link can be worth having and still leave its members somewhere you have to go and look. The
Wide Family panel is where sharing is *decided*, and a member you consult every day does not
belong on the page about consent any more than your own members do.

So among everyone a linked family shares with you, **you pick which ones are siblings**, and
a sibling is treated almost as one of your own: they appear in the summary, in every column
set — possessions, professions, activity — wherever your own members are listed. In those
lists they are drawn as a **sub-section of the realm they are on**, under the family they
belong to:

    Thunderstrike
      your member
      your other member
      Linked family
        their member
      Another linked family
        their member

Grouped by realm first, because the realm is the fact that decides whether two characters can
hand each other anything; grouped by family second, because whose they are is never in doubt
and never merged away (§"What linked data is, and is not"). A sibling is never counted in your
totals: the money on the totals line is your family's money, and adding somebody else's to it
would produce a figure that describes nobody.

**Naming a sibling is a decision about your own screen, and nothing else.** It moves no data,
sends no message, and asks nobody. It cannot: a sibling is chosen from the members a family
has *already* granted you, so there is nothing left to consent to — the sharing decision was
taken, by them, before the name could be offered. This is exactly why it needs no bilateral
approval, and the reason is worth stating rather than leaving as an omission: everywhere else
in §6 the answer is "both sides must agree", and this is the one place where the question has
already been answered.

Withdrawing a grant removes the member, so it removes the sibling with it. Nothing has to be
tidied up by hand.

### Constraints worth designing around

Addon messages travel over the game's channels — whisper, guild, party. They are rate
limited, so a large family takes time to transfer and the interface reports progress rather
than appearing to hang. An exchange interrupted by a logout is abandoned after a timeout
rather than waiting forever, and whatever arrived before the interruption is kept.

Whether two players can exchange addon messages at all depends on whether the game will
carry a whisper between them, which is not true of every pairing on every client. Where it
is not, the guild channel is the fallback for guildmates, and there is no fallback for
anyone else. This is a property of the game and Family says so plainly rather than appearing
broken (§11).

## 7. Guild share

A lighter Wide Family: **it carries much less, and it carries it with much more automation.**
Those two facts are the same decision. Wide Family asks a player to approve one member and one
category at a time because it can reach anything a member owns; Guild share carries a small
fixed handful of facts that are already visible to everyone it is shared with, and asks
nobody's approval for them. One thing is offered beyond that handful — professions — and it
is the exception that has a grid, for the reason §7.1 sets out.

**What it carries, and nothing else:** for a guildmate and for every one of their characters
who is also in this guild — class, race, level, **both talent specialisations**, and **equipped
gear with its average item level**.

Talents cross as the *shape* of the build — which trees, how many points in each, which of the
two they are in — and not talent by talent. That is a limit imposed by the channel rather than
by consent: a full pair of trees is some kilobytes per character, times a player's alts, times
everybody in the guild running Family, down a channel shared with every other addon they run.
It is also exactly what the panel draws. Wanting somebody's build talent by talent is a Wide
Family link, where it goes to one person who asked for it.

**Never:** bags, bank, mail, quests, money, auctions, reputations, achievements. Not "not yet"
— those belong to Wide Family and require its consent. Wanting a guildmate's bag contents is a
perfectly reasonable thing to want; it is a Wide Family link, and it is one on purpose.

Professions were on that list and are no longer. What took them off is not a change of mind
about where the line is: it is that they came back with a grid in front of them, and §7.1 is
that grid.

**Why the list above needs no consent grid.** Everything in it is what the game already shows any
guildmate who targets you and presses Inspect. Family is not disclosing it; it is saving both
of you the trip, across characters who are not standing in front of you and at hours when
neither of you is online. A consent dialogue in front of a fact the game gives away for free
teaches players to click through consent dialogues, which is the opposite of what §6's grid is
for. **One switch turns it on, and the same switch turns it off in both directions at once:**
a client with Guild share disabled neither asks nor answers.

It ships off, like Wide Family. Not because consent requires it — the argument above stands —
but because a first release that begins talking to a guild on somebody's behalf before they
have asked is a poor introduction, whatever it is saying. What matters is that the switch is
findable. Both sharing panels are in the strip whether their feature is on or off — Wide
Family's used to appear only once its feature was already on, and a choice nobody can find is
not a choice anybody has made. Both switches are in Options, together and nowhere else, so a
player looks in one place rather than hunting the panel each feature governs; a panel whose
feature is off says so and points at them. Neither switch needs a reload.

### The Guild tab

The guild roster, with a filter for **online only** or **everyone**. Each row carries a dot
saying whether that player is running Family — filled for yes, empty for no. Guildmates not
running it show no talents, no gear and no version, and nothing on your side changes that;
that is the ordinary state of a guild where you are the only user, and the panel says so
rather than looking broken.

Clicking a guildmate opens what is known about them: each of their guild characters, their
talents in both specialisations, and their gear with its item level.

**Alts outside the guild are not offered, and there is no option to add them.** Guild share is
scoped to the guild, and a scope with a switch to widen it is not a scope. Somebody's characters
elsewhere are a Wide Family link.

### When it happens

Whoever is online is asked, and answers, on the same terms as §6: announce on login, answer an
announcement, and *Update now* whenever a person wants it. **Once seen, it is kept** — a
guildmate who logged off an hour ago is still shown, with the age of the record on it, exactly
as everything else in Family is (§2.2). Nothing is fetched from somebody who is offline,
because there is nobody there to fetch it from.

An announcement from somebody whose record we already hold is not worth a second copy of it,
so the exchange is skipped — but **it is never skipped in silence.** A hello is answered with
a hello, whispered to the one person and marked as a reply so that nothing answers it back.
Being heard from is the only way one client knows another runs Family at all, and what this
end holds from somebody says nothing about what that end holds from us: a player who cleared
their saved variables would otherwise announce to a guild of clients that each decided, on
their own, that there was nothing to say, and read every one of them as not running Family.

### 7.1 Shared professions, and who can craft what

*"Which guildie can craft this?"* is asked in guild chat several times an evening, and it is
answered by whoever happens to be awake and remembers. The person who could actually make the
thing is usually offline, and so is the alt of theirs that has the profession. That is the
question this answers, and the reason it is worth carrying something the rest of §7 refuses.

**It is offered one character and one profession at a time, and nothing is offered until it
is.** Each of your characters in the guild lists its professions, and each of those is ticked
separately: only Leatherworking on one, only Tailoring on another, Alchemy and Blacksmithing
on a third. A grid with nothing ticked is the state Family arrives in, and it shares nothing.

**A tick is one decision and carries three things together** — the rank, what that profession
can make, and its cooldowns. Splitting them into separate ticks was considered and refused:
it doubles the grid in exchange for a distinction almost nobody holds an opinion about, and a
grid that is tedious to read is a grid that gets agreed to without being read, which is the
same failure §7 avoids by not putting a dialogue in front of gear.

**Why this one has a grid when gear does not.** Inspect shows a guildmate's gear and talents,
so a dialogue in front of those teaches players to click through dialogues. Inspect shows
nobody's recipe list. The two answers are one answer applied to two different facts, and the
grid is where the difference is visible. Nothing about it weakens the case above it; it is
what that case predicts.

**What crosses is identifiers, and never names — whichever identifier the client gave.** A
shared recipe travels as the spell it is and as the item it makes, and it travels when it has
*either* of them: a French guild and a German one then answer each other's questions without
either having recorded a word the other can read (§2.1), and the answer appears on a crafted
item as well as on a pattern. **A recipe the client names in neither way is not shared**, and
the panel says how many were left out rather than quietly sharing fewer than it appears to.

*Amended 2026-08-30.* This asked for the spell and treated the item as an extra, which is the
shape of some clients and not of all of them: `GetTradeSkillRecipeLink` returns nothing at all
on Classic Era, where a character's recipes carry an item id apiece and no spell whatever, and
the Craft frame on that same client is the mirror image. Written the first way, guild crafters
shared nothing on Era except enchanting. Both ids are read, either will carry a recipe, and
neither is assumed — which is the rule `DATASOURCES.md` already drew from the same
measurement.

**Ranks alone are an honest answer and are treated as one.** A profession that has been ticked
but whose window has never been opened shares a rank and nothing else, and that guildmate is
reported as *may know it* — never as unable. This is §2.2 in a place where getting it wrong
would send somebody to the wrong person.

**Two ages compose, and the older one is what the answer carries.** A guild recipe list is as
old as the last time that character opened that profession, and as old as the last time we
heard from that player. What is shown is the older of the two, because an answer is only as
current as the least current thing it was built from.

**Scope is unchanged.** Only characters in the guild may be offered, exactly as the rest of §7
has it. A crafting alt outside the guild is a Wide Family link, and a scope with a switch to
widen it is not a scope.

### What a grant is attached to

**A grant is made to a guild, not to the world.** It is keyed by the guild as well as by the
character and the profession, so it means what it said when it was made: *this guild may see
this*. That has two consequences and both of them are the point.

**A character who leaves the guild stops sharing.** Not "stops being added to the next
message" — stops, and what was already sent is replaced by what is sent next, which no
longer contains them. Everything one player sends replaces everything held from that player
rather than being merged into it, so a character who has stopped sharing is dropped at the
other end without anything having to go and delete them. Joining a different guild does not
resume it either: the new guild was never the guild that was agreed to, and a grant that
follows its owner around is not a grant anybody made.

**They are dropped when the next offering arrives, which is not when the withdrawal was
made.** *Amended 2026-08-30, having been stated unconditionally.* Two clients can only talk
while both are online and there is no offline mailbox, so a withdrawal reaches each person
holding the old copy at the moment those two next overlap — not at the moment it is
withdrawn. Until then the profession that was withdrawn is still answerable on their client.
No design can do better: a withdrawal cannot reach somebody who is not there. What can be
done is to bound how long it runs, and it is bounded at **fourteen days** — everything held
from a player nobody has heard from for that long is dropped, and rebuilds itself from one
exchange if they ever come back.

**That number is a decision about consent and not about disk**, which is why it is here and
not among the housekeeping. It is short because the two failures are not symmetrical:
dropping too early costs a single exchange the next time those two meet, while keeping too
long is a window nobody can close — not the person who withdrew, not the person holding it.
Fourteen days is longer than any ordinary absence, so nobody active is ever dropped; past it
a player has stopped playing that character, which is exactly the case whose withdrawal can
never arrive by any other route.

**Two withdrawals reach nobody, ever, and saying so is part of the promise.** A character
removed from the guild while its owner is not playing it, and a character that is deleted. In
both, the owner's own client goes on offering what it still believes, and every other client
faithfully repeats it — so no exchange corrects it, because the correction would have to come
from the owner and the owner is the one who is wrong. Neither the replacement above nor the
bound above reaches those two: the first faithfully replaces the wrong list with the same
wrong list, and the second is about an owner who has gone rather than one who is playing
every day. The guild's own event log is the only thing that knows, and whether Family reads
it is open.

**A player who leaves the guild stops seeing what the guild shared.** What was held for a
guild is dropped when it is no longer their guild, rather than sitting on disk to be
resurrected stale if they ever rejoin. This is the half that Family can actually keep, and it
is worth being plain about the half it cannot: the other end is somebody else's computer
running somebody else's copy, and no addon compels it. What Family promises is that it will
not send what was not granted and will ask the other side to forget what was withdrawn. That
is a promise honestly kept on this side, not a lock, and it is as true here as in §6.

### Cooldowns

A transmute, a bolt of mooncloth, a salt shaker: the things a guild most often needs and the
things nobody remembers the state of. **Cooldowns cross for shared professions and for no
others**, which follows from a tick meaning one thing rather than three.

They travel as **the time remaining**, and are stored on arrival as the moment they come ready.
Two clients need not agree on an epoch, and a duration is right whoever reads it; storing it as
a moment is still the rule everywhere in Family, because a countdown written down yesterday
is wrong today and a moment is still true.

The distinction Family already draws between a craft's cooldown and an item's is carried
across unchanged. A craft is used through a window Family watches, so a transmute reading
*ready* is evidence. An item is used out of the bags where Family sees nothing, so an item
cooldown crosses only while it is still running, and never as a claim that it is
available.

**They appear beside the crafter and not as a board of their own.** For anything with a
cooldown, "who can make this" is the wrong question and "whose is up" is the right one, so the
answer sorts a guildmate whose transmute is ready above one whose is seven hours out. A
standing list of everything ready across a guild is a reasonable thing to want and is not this;
it can be added later without changing anything here.

### What this is not

It is not a directory of crafters for hire, it does not price anything, it does not rank people
by who is best to ask, and it does not advise. It reports who is known to be able to make
a thing and how old that knowledge is, and stops — §2.5, in the one feature where the
temptation to go further is strongest.

### Traffic, and how it stays affordable

A recipe list is the largest thing §7 has ever carried, and it is carried once rather than
every login. Each shared profession announces **how many recipes it has and a fingerprint of
them**; the list itself is asked for only when that fingerprint differs from what this end
already holds. A settled guild therefore costs nothing after the day everybody met, which is
what makes "once seen, it is kept" affordable here as well.

Withdrawing a profession changes the fingerprint, so a withdrawal is never mistaken for
something already held and skipped. That is deliberate and it is the same promise §6 makes:
the half of an exchange that takes something away does not wait for anybody to press a button.

**Professions travel as their own message with their own schema version.** A client too old
to understand them still exchanges gear and talents with one that is not, rather than both
ends dropping every message the other sends. Two versions degrade to *that cannot be read
here*, named, exactly as §6 requires — and never to a silence that looks like a guild where
nobody runs Family.

### Where the answer appears

In two places, because there are two ways the question gets asked, and in no third place:

- **On the item's own tooltip**, under the crafters from your own family. Hovering a pattern
  in the auction house, or the thing it makes, is where the question is actually asked.
  The list is capped and ordered — knows it first, online first — because a tooltip that
  fills the screen has answered a different question than the one asked.
- **In the recipe search that already exists**, as a second group beside your own family's
  crafters, labelled as the guild's. One box, one question, two sources.

The Guild tab keeps being about people: who runs Family, and what each of their characters is
wearing. **The grid of what you share lives there**, next to the roster it governs, in the same
way Wide Family's grid lives on Wide Family's panel — a switch belongs in Options and a grid
does not, because a grid is the feature rather than a preference about it.

**The answer is a character, and naming the player as well would add nothing.** Guild records
are keyed by whoever sent them, so the player is known — but everything §7 shares is a
character *in this guild*, which is the scope stated above and not a detail. The crafter is
therefore on the same roster the reader is looking at: whisperable if they are online, and
visibly not if they are not. A second name would be somebody the reader has to go and look up
in order to get back to the first.

*Settled 2026-08-30, having first been written the other way round.* The argument for naming
the player rested on the alt being "somebody who is not there", and that is false for a guild:
you may be in one guild at a time, so a shared character is a roster entry like any other. It
is not false for Wide Family, where a linked family's members are on other realms and other
rosters with no way to whisper them or see whether they are online — which is why that feature
names families and this one names characters.

### Built in three slices

Each is recorded, stored and shown before the next begins, and each is worth having on its own:

1. **Ranks.** The grid, and profession with rank crossing the wire. Answers *may know it* and
   *not enough skill yet* on the tooltip. Small enough to ride with what §7 already sends.
2. **Recipes.** The list of ids, the fingerprint, and the search. This is the slice that
   answers the question the feature is named for.
3. **Cooldowns.** What is ready, beside whoever it is ready for.

### The two are independent

A guild share and a Wide Family link know nothing about each other and neither implies the
other. If you want a guildmate to see more of one of your members than gear and talents, you
link families and grant it — and it appears where linked data appears, in Wide Family and in
the members you have made siblings, never in the guild panel. The guild panel shows guild
data. Two routes to a fact would mean two places to look for it and two places to withdraw
it, and the second of those is what makes it a defect rather than a duplication.

The guild bank is not part of this. It is scanned by whoever opens it (§3) and shown in
Possessions, where the other containers are.

## 8. Locales

Family fully supports every language Blizzard ships a Classic client in, which covers the EU
realms — English, German, French, Spanish and Russian — for both the interface and the
recorded data.

**Italian is deliberately not on that list.** Blizzard has never shipped an Italian client
for any Classic product, so there is no Italian client to support and no Italian strings to
translate against.

The data half is free, and it is free because of §2.1: identifiers are language-neutral, so
a member recorded on a German client reads back correctly on a French one, and a linked
family from a third language needs no translation at all. There is no locale-specific
storage and no per-language saved file.

The interface half is one string table per language, in `addons/Family/Locales/`. **The key
is the English sentence** — `L["Last seen"]` — so there is no vocabulary of invented key names
to keep in step, and anything untranslated falls through to readable English rather than to a
missing-key marker. An empty string in a locale file means *deliberately left English*, which
is not the same as absent.

**Words the game already has come from the game.** A gear slot, a reputation standing, a
class name, the glyph list: `Family:GameWord` takes Blizzard's own string and falls back to
Family's translation only where the client has none. That is right in all eleven languages
rather than the five here, and — the part that matters — it is the same word the rest of the
player's interface is using on the same screen. A term is not Family's to abbreviate, whatever
its length.

**Layout gives way to the language, never the other way round.** Every fixed width in the tree
was chosen by looking at English, which is the shortest of the five. A column too narrow for
its own heading is widened and the room is taken from whichever columns have the most slack;
no column is ever left narrower than its own heading (`UI:FitColumns`). Widths are measured
with `GetStringWidth` rather than counted, because the fonts are not monospaced and Blizzard's
translation of a term is not something this repository can predict.

Anything Family counts is a whole clause chosen by the count, never a stem with an `s`
appended: that is a plural in English and in none of the other four, and Russian has three
forms.

The harness reads every locale file on every client, whatever the language, and refuses
orphaned keys, translations too long for the space they sit in, and format specifiers that do
not match their English. It also refuses any language claimed in `CURSEFORGE.md` or in this
section that has no translations behind it (L-013).

**A word a client wrote is not an identity.** Two things Family shows are named by the client
rather than by an id it can store — professions on Classic Era, which the skill list gives no
id for, and races, whose id `UnitRace` does return but which were being shown from the word
instead. Both are keyed by a language-neutral identity and named at display from a table
generated out of the client's own DB2 (`addons/Family/SkillLines.lua`, `addons/Family/Races.lua`;
see DATASOURCES §3), so a member recorded on a German client reads correctly on a French one
and a member shared by a third family reads correctly to both.

Where the recording client's own word is in the reader's language it is preferred to the
table, because the game gendered it and no table here can: a Russian gnome is a Gnom or a
Gnomka and the client already knew which.

**A sentence is translated or it is half a sentence.** A missing translation falling back to
English is the right failure for a label standing on its own, and the wrong one for anything
Family prints as prose: the values inside a printed line — a date, a sum of money, a
profession — come from the same helpers the panels use and are translated whatever the
sentence around them is. So an untranslated diagnosis read *"seen il y a 19j, 2 container(s),
meta says jamais"*. Everything the slash commands print is translated in all four languages,
and the harness refuses a new one that is not.

The search index is per language and is rebuilt when the client language changes (§2.1).

## 9. Limits worth knowing

None of these are faults. They follow from how Family gets its information, and knowing them
saves looking for a bug that is not there.

**It only knows what it has been shown.** There is no way to ask the server about a member
you are not logged into. A bank never opened is not there; a profession never opened has no
recipes.

**Family starts empty, and there is no import.** However many years of history another alt
manager has accumulated on your account, Family begins with nothing and fills as you play
each member once. This is a deliberate choice rather than an omission: everything Family
holds, it holds because it watched it happen. The first login on each member costs a few
seconds and buys everything in §3.

**Some things are recorded only while a window is open.** Bank, guild bank, profession
recipes and their difficulty colours, mailbox, auction house. If a member skills up and the
profession window is not reopened, the counts stay as they were.

**Bags are the exception.** Rescanned after every login and on every change.

**Cooldowns are known only from when their window was open.** *No cooldown seen* is not
*no cooldown* (§2.2).

**Wide Family depends on the other player.** Both online, both running a compatible version,
and everything granted deliberately. A snapshot, not a subscription.

**Guild data depends on other people running Family.** Guildmates who do not are invisible
to it, and nothing on your side changes that.

**A replacement crafting interface changes what "select this recipe" means.** Clicking a recipe
in Family selects it in the profession window that is open, through the client's own
`SelectTradeSkill` / `SelectCraft`. An addon that replaces that window — Skillet and its like —
draws its own list, and the selection may not show there even though it was made. Measured on a
live client with Skillet installed: a plain click appeared to do nothing and a shift-click on
the profession button selected the recipe as intended. This is not a fault on either side, and
it is worth knowing before an evening is spent looking for one in Family.

**Mail posted to a member is a claim about the post, not about their mailbox.** It is
recorded as sent, marked as such, and replaced by the truth the moment that member opens
their own mailbox (§5).

**Prices and auctions are a snapshot** from the last visit, not a live feed.

**Records are kept for characters that no longer exist** until you remove them. The
right-click menu on the summary clears a member, a realm or an account.

**Each client keeps its own records.** Era, Burning Crusade and Mists are separate
installations with separate saved files, and Family does not merge them.

## 10. What Family does that nothing does today

Recorded so the project can be measured against it.

1. **Wide Family.** Linking two players' families, with per-member and per-category consent
   and a refresh that either side can trigger. Nothing available does this: the closest
   thing is a one-shot snapshot of a whole account, all or nothing.
2. **Talent comparison.** Two members side by side, including across a link.
3. **Siblings.** Somebody else's characters sitting in your own summary, under their own
   family's name, because you decided they belong there and they decided you may see them
   (§6). Two consents, taken separately, one screen.
4. **The whole family's gear on one screen** (§4.3.1). Every alt manager can show you a
   character sheet. The question people actually have is which alt is behind, and that is
   not answerable one sheet at a time.
5. **Mail written down as it is posted** (§5), so a member's row is right before that member
   has logged in — and honest about which half of that it knows.
6. **Real locale independence**, by storing identifiers rather than names (§2.1, §8) —
   which is also what makes a cross-language link work.
7. ~~**Advice about what to learn, not only what to craft.**~~ Withdrawn 2026-08-09 (§2.5).
   Family reports; it does not advise. The ground is still unclaimed and Family is not
   claiming it.
8. **A window built for forty members.** Searchable member lists, collapsible groups, and a
   recap that answers *what needs attention* without reading the table.
9. **Mists as a first-class client**, designed in rather than bolted on.
10. **Two addons and one saved file**, with a schema version and a migration that runs — not
    a family of modules with a format that can break silently between versions.
11. **Sharing that fails visibly.** Named tables and versioned payloads, so a version
    mismatch is a message rather than a corrupted record.
12. **Lazy, compressed storage.** Member records decoded on demand rather than every record
    parsed at every login.
13. **Stated staleness everywhere** (§2.2).

## 12. Build order

One domain end to end — record it, store it, show it — rather than a horizontal layer. A
single vertical slice proves the architecture; a finished data layer with no interface
proves nothing and shows nothing for months.

The first slice is **bags and money on the summary**: scanning on change, the compressed
per-member record, the schema and its migration, the name-resolution layer, and the summary
table with one column set. Every architectural decision in §2 is exercised by it, and it is
useful on the day it works.
